# Direct Play and external playback

Choose the preferred add action in **Settings → Prefer Direct Play**. The add sheet always offers both Download and Direct Play after the torrent file list is ready. Download continues to save selected files to Downloads/Webtor. Direct Play creates no full-sized video backing files.

Settings offers Auto RAM or a manual video cache of 64, 128, 256, 512 or 1,024 MiB per transfer. The same limit controls the download write-back cache. The saved choice applies to active and future transfers; lowering the limit lets normal piece replacement release excess buffers safely. Preview tasks retain their separate memory budget. Starting another Direct Play session releases the previous torrent's RAM session, while keeping its library entry, resume position and startup cache. Native readers are limited to four per Direct Play torrent. Settings also allows 8–500 peer connections per transfer, with 55 as the default.

**Auto RAM** chooses 64–512 MiB per transfer from Android's total and available memory, its low-memory threshold, and the number of active transfers. It reserves memory for Android and other work, and uses 64 MiB on low-RAM devices or under memory pressure. Auto is the default when no manual cache preference exists; existing manual choices are preserved. The app rechecks the budget on startup, on returning to the foreground, and before configuring a transfer. An active Direct Play torrent that needs larger pieces retains its working limit rather than accepting an unusable reduction. The external player can keep its own read buffer independently of this torrent cache.

Opening and container metadata pieces receive urgent priority. Up to 8 MiB of verified startup pieces per torrent is saved privately under `.startup`; inactive caches are pruned with a 32 MiB total target. Opening another episode moves the startup budget to that episode. All remaining video bytes stay in RAM. The asynchronous startup writer can hold up to another 8 MiB of queued verified payload; peer buffers and engine metadata consume additional memory. A torrent whose pieces exceed one quarter of the RAM cache must use Download.

Warm startup pieces are checksum-validated before reuse, including torrent SHA-1 verification for v1 torrents. Validation and native piece hashing use the Go runtime’s available CPU allowance; the torrent client retains a shared cap on active hashers across torrents. Saved-data verification now pipelines independent pieces rather than awaiting them one at a time. Peer network I/O continues using Go's asynchronous runtime and the existing anacrolix scheduler.

The forward buffer adjusts to observed sequential read consumption and available RAM. Seeks retarget urgent pieces immediately. Idle descriptors park after 15 seconds and resume on the next read; in-flight reads are never parked. A blocked read can retry twice within a 45-second total wait, refreshing its urgent request through the current scheduler. This recovery is experimental: it does not add TorrServe's duplicate-block request algorithm. Sparse swarms and slow peers can still require buffering.

**Settings → Experimental → Adaptive buffer** is an optional feedback experiment, off by default. It adds look-ahead for variable external-player read demand using a smoothed mean and variance, then reduces that headroom as demand settles. Damping limits each adjustment and a dead band avoids small repeated resizes. The existing startup, seek and RAM-budget limits still apply, and the setting is captured when you press Play. The idea is inspired by feedback and scaling; it does not implement a mathematical universality theorem or promise faster swarm downloads. Correctness tests cover varying demand and real local-peer playback within a 16 MiB cache; public-swarm stall reduction has not been measured.

**Burst-aware buffer** is a separate opt-in experiment inspired by patterns across multiple timescales. It adds fast and slow demand estimates to the feedback controller so a short calm period does not immediately erase a longer burst history. It works independently and includes the Adaptive buffer feedback; with both switches on, the burst-aware policy is used. This is a practical multiscale heuristic, not a formal fractal estimator. Both buffering switches are off by default and apply to the next Play request. Default, adaptive, burst-aware and combined configurations are checked against the same local-peer playback and seek test with a 16 MiB RAM cache.

**Peer exploration** is an opt-in foraging-inspired heuristic for active transfers. Every ten seconds it samples connected peers' useful download and upload counters. When the connection limit is full and another discovered candidate is waiting, it can release one connection that has transferred no useful payload for at least 90 seconds. It preserves productive peers, new connections, the only connected peer and the sole known seed. At most one slot rotates per torrent every 30 seconds. The existing discovery, connection and dial limits remain in charge. The switch applies immediately, survives app restarts, and resets its observation history when changed. It is not a contextual-bandit learner and makes no throughput guarantee.

All four performance controls can be enabled together: Instant play boost, Adaptive buffer, Burst-aware buffer and Peer exploration. The combined local-peer test downloads and seeks through a 40 MiB payload with a 16 MiB cache while the peer-exploration policy is enabled and sampled. Burst-aware mode includes Adaptive feedback, boost enlarges the startup and maximum look-ahead targets, and peer exploration acts on connection slots; the storage budget remains the final limit. This verifies the tested combination's correctness, not a speedup on every network.

To compare the experiments, keep the torrent, player, RAM limit, peer cap, Instant play boost and network the same. Try all three off, then Adaptive buffer only, Burst-aware buffer only, and Peer exploration only. Compare time to first playback, stall count and duration, transfer speed and memory over several runs. Give Peer exploration at least two minutes with a full connection pool and waiting candidates. Network and swarm changes can outweigh differences between policies.

The Direct Play pieces grid shows current RAM residency. Verified pieces in RAM fill green, resident pieces still arriving show amber, and absent or evicted pieces are hollow. A saved startup copy on disk does not keep an evicted RAM block filled. Larger torrents group several pieces in each block; the filled fraction follows the resident piece count. The engine reuses one compressed grid snapshot per torrent until the buffer changes and signals these changes through the existing UI event clock.

Direct Play waits for verified storage pieces through cancellation-aware events, copying each piece before eviction can replace its bytes. Large range reads advance the playhead window while reading. Reader shutdown cancels and joins active torrent I/O before closing its storage cursor. The pinned anacrolix v1.61.0 dependency includes only the upstream [peer-writer wakeup fix](https://github.com/anacrolix/torrent/commit/aa71e8d2f5c439257c0313919d671fd087347233) and its regression test; see [patch provenance](../engine-go/patches/torrent/WEBTOR_PATCH.md).

Closing the external player retains one roughly 2 MiB resume range (rounded to torrent pieces) for up to 90 seconds, within the existing cache budget. Reopened readers start their scheduling window near the recent body read; metadata probes cannot replace that hint. When a compatible player returns its position and duration, playback also prefetches an estimated byte position before launch. The player still controls the actual timestamp seek, including variable-bitrate media. Pause, removal and shutdown release the hold. Completed downloads open locally without waiting on unrelated saved-data checks, and repeated playback skips redundant MediaStore metadata refreshes.

Direct Play library entries load paused with no assumed RAM progress. Reopening the app does not restore or prebuffer them automatically. **Clear cache** deletes imported temporary torrents and inactive startup caches while preserving active playback and download scratch storage. Deleting a Direct Play entry with the erase action also removes its startup cache.

External playback uses Android content URI grants. Compatible MX-style players receive a naturally ordered selected-video playlist and matching selected subtitle sidecars. VLC also receives its subtitle-location extra. Resume positions are saved only when the external player returns a supported numeric position; playlist returns must identify the episode before its position is recorded. Other players may ignore these extras or keep their own bookmarks. Video playback buttons always show **Play**, including when a saved position is passed to the player. The episode three-dot menu and separate play-from-start action have been removed. Subtitles must be selected with the video; embedded subtitle controls remain in the external player.

**Settings → Playback method** selects **Standard** or **HTTP stream (experimental)** as the saved method for Play on incomplete videos and Direct Play. HTTP hands the player the existing loopback range stream; Standard uses the content URI. Completed downloads keep the direct local-file path. Episode rows keep one compact playback button visible. See the [HTTP comparison notes](HTTP_PLAYER_EXPLORATION.md) for caching, lifecycle behavior, validation and device-test limits.

Validation covers startup corruption, RAM eviction without fabricated bytes, asynchronous close, active-cache preservation, episode switching, idle reader wakeup and stalled-read retry. A loopback peer test downloads and seeks through a 40 MiB payload while enforcing a 16 MiB video cache. These are correctness checks, not measurements of first-play latency on a phone or a public swarm.
